# Workshop Part 1 — The Game Idea (Spielidee)

> **Source:** Making Games Magazin 03/2010, pp. 34–38 · **Author:** Daniel Dumont
> **Use:** Background for skills supporting `01_GameIdea.md`

---

## TL;DR

Stage 1 of 3 (Game Idea → Exposé → Game Concept). Length: **½ – 1 A4 page**. Job: high-level vision that triggers in the reader the same emotion the author feels. Cover seven components, none in depth. Note detail-level thoughts but do **not** pursue them.

---

## Agent Cheat Sheet

### Required components (cover all seven)

| # | Component | Required content | Max length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Genre | Established genre name, OR closest existing genres + famous reference | 1–2 sentences |
| 2 | Plot | Setting, narrative background, story-evolution yes/no, theme | 2–4 sentences |
| 3 | Core Mechanic preview | One-sentence statement of recurring player action | 1 sentence |
| 4 | Appeal / Wow Factor | Surface quality graspable in seconds | 1–2 sentences |
| 5 | USPs | 1–3 differentiators vs. competition | 1–3 bullets |
| 6 | Out-of-scope | What is deliberately not part of the vision | optional |
| 7 | Open points | Assumptions still uncertain | optional |

### Stage gate rules

- Do not advance to Exposé until: (a) discussed with others, (b) ambiguities resolved, (c) viable on current knowledge.
- Inside this stage: only details of **comparable depth** (Principle of Balanced Detail Depths).
- Detail-level ideas surfacing while writing: **note them, do not pursue**.

### Length / format

- Target: ½ to 1 A4 page total.
- Prose, integrated. **Not** a bullet checklist.
- Use **visual descriptions** (reference real places + deltas, reference existing games).

### Anti-patterns

| Failure mode | Diagnostic | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Plot-only description | No paragraph on what the player *does* | Add core mechanic preview |
| Drilling one feature in depth | One section >> others | Pull back; flag detail for later |
| Multi-page USP list | More than 3 USPs or >½ page | Cut to 1–3 short bullets; move to Exposé §2 |
| Generic wow factor ("nice graphics") | Could apply to any game | Force specificity in seconds-graspable terms |
| Author can't state emotion the game evokes | Vague excitement only | Run objectivity test: does a stranger feel it too? |

### Diagnostic questions (agent must ask)

1. What genre or genre-mix? Which existing games anchor the mental model?
2. What does the player *do* repeatedly? (mechanic preview)
3. Does the plot just evolve, or does it shape gameplay?
4. What hooks a viewer in ≤10 seconds of watching?
5. What is genuinely different vs. the dominant title in this genre?
6. What emotion does the writer want the reader to feel? Does the draft trigger it?

### Genre-mix discipline

When user names "X meets Y":
- Identify inherited mechanic from X
- Identify inherited mechanic from Y
- Probe: how do they combine in one moment of gameplay?

### Worked example — Darkstar One (Dumont's own)

> *"Darkstar One is a space-adventure game in the style of Elite, Privateer 2, or Freelancer. In a universe that is very large by today's standards, the player takes on the role of an adventurer and flies through space in cockpit view of their space glider, living through an adventure story. They fight space battles that are controlled like a 3D shooter. Their ever-growing demands on ship and equipment are initially funded by simple, action-oriented missions or free trade — later in the game increasingly through piracy, bounty hunting, and more demanding missions. Via hyperspace jumps the player moves from solar system to solar system, can fight space battles or land on orbital stations, trade, accept missions, and conduct dialogues. The entry into Darkstar One is kept very simple: directly after the intro the user receives their first story-relevant mission and is thus pulled into the game."*

Notice:
- **Anchored** by three reference games (Elite, Privateer 2, Freelancer) and one genre (3D shooter)
- **Only three mechanics** named (exploration, shooter combat, ship progression) — others exist in the finished game but are omitted from the Idea

## Reference: the three stages of conception

1. **Game Idea (Spielidee)** — high-level vision
2. **Exposé** — concise overview across all perspectives
3. **Game Concept (Konzept)** — implementation-grade documentation, split into **Functional Design** (what the player sees/does) and **Logical Concept** (internal rules). Functional Design is written first because it is more concrete.

## Reference: Principle of Balanced Detail Depths

Inside one stage, only deal with details of **comparable depth**. Drilling into one feature during the Game Idea stage prevents a broad viable vision from forming. Cover the **breadth** first, increase depth uniformly in later stages.
